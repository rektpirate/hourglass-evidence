## The seven items in the room

Each item: the BlueLabs side, the LAPS side, the shared ground, and what the two could build together. The first six come from the research handoff's overlap table. The seventh is a reading of the two handoffs together.

### 1. Protecting people who can't protect themselves

- BlueLabs: fighting for those who can't fend for themselves; dignity and sovereign connection.
- LAPS: the person is the central value; children "the most precious good", with a right to be happy and to be heard (Code of Ethics 2018; UN Convention on the Rights of the Child as inspiration).
- Shared ground: both start from the person who has the least power in the room.
- Together: a shared statement of principles for any joint project, written from the Code of Ethics and BlueLabs' beliefs, before any tool is named.

### 2. Getting a person to real human help

- BlueLabs: a human always in the loop; mechanism, not mind.
- LAPS: asking for help as part of recovery (the founder has spoken publicly about addiction, recovery and asking for help; Verissimo, Sep 2020).
- Shared ground: technology's job is to reach a person, never to replace one.
- Together: every BlueLabs tool that LAPS touches shows a human route to help first, the way CaseLock's demo already does ("If you are in danger now, call 000").

### 3. Recovery

- BlueLabs: suicide prevention, paced over 12–24 months; people in recovery as a group it serves.
- LAPS: minors with addiction among its special-needs groups; the founder's openness about his own recovery.
- Shared ground: recovery as a long path with people around it, not a moment.
- Together: the recovery feed blur and the paced approach, offered to LAPS's programmes for young people. Our reading: this is the item LAPS is most likely to recognise from its own work.

### 4. Safety that leads to autonomy

- BlueLabs: HOURGLASS, where people control access to their own data.
- LAPS: Casa LAPS Cascais, opened 2 Oct 2026: nine suites, stays up to six months, 24-hour security and psychological support, with autonomy as the aim, not only safety (ANSA 2 Oct 2026; Euronews 3 Oct 2026).
- Shared ground: safety is the start; the goal is a person back in charge of her own life and her own records.
- Together: HOURGLASS-style control of personal records for women leaving Casa LAPS: what is shared, with whom, for how long, decided by her.

### 5. Young people's digital harm

- BlueLabs: the kids' phone filter; the recovery feed blur.
- LAPS: digital-addiction projects for young people; the foundation quit social media in 2017.
- Shared ground: both treat the feed as a hazard for young people, not a neutral channel.
- Together: a pilot of the phone filter with a LAPS educational partner, measured by output and outcome, not by downloads.

### 6. Verifiable impact records

- BlueLabs: facts, not hopium; a ledger that binds its author too.
- LAPS: no published accounts; the site's reports page is missing; impact given as output counts only (6,000 minors and 600 teachers 2017–19, no method); Deloitte certification claimed but unverified.
- Shared ground: a gap on one side and a tool on the other.
- Together: LAPS's programme outputs and outcomes recorded in a ledger that LAPS cannot quietly rewrite and BlueLabs cannot either, published as a page anyone can check. Our reading: the most concrete first project, because it needs no change to what LAPS already does.

### 7. An evidence vault for women leaving a shelter (our reading)

- BlueLabs: CaseLock v2, a vault on her own phone opened by Face ID or Touch ID only; a sealed copy for a lawyer or the police opened with a share phrase, never her passkey; a Notes cover and a calculator cover; a demo that calls no one.
- LAPS: Casa LAPS Cascais, a shelter for women and children escaping domestic violence, with plans for an international network.
- Shared ground: a woman leaving violence needs her evidence kept safe, hers alone, and ready for a lawyer or the police when she chooses.
- Together: CaseLock offered to Casa LAPS residents, in Portuguese and Italian, with the shelter's legal partners as the sealed-copy recipients. This item is not in the research notes; it is a reading of the CaseLock handoff and the LAPS research together, and the card says so.
